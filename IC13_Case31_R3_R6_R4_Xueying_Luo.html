Introduction and data decisions

Employees Federal Credit Union (EFCU) surveyed members because it wanted to understand members’ experiences and how well its services were meeting their needs. This report uses the 128 records in the provided cleaned SPSS file and addresses R3, R6, and the three parts of R4. R4 compares residents in the headquarters area (57 people) with members living elsewhere (71 people), based on Q38. Findings describe associations within this survey, not causes.

The assignment previously required reliability and factor analyses. Their results are not repeated here. For employee proficiency, the dataset already provides att_employee, the row average of Q3–Q6; the four constituent items are also shown separately. Operational effectiveness is reported item by item, rather than as an unvalidated overall index, consistent with the prior IC11-2 analysis. Missing answers are excluded from the particular analysis involved.

R3 — Members’ views of employee proficiency

R3 — Descriptive statistics

Q3–Q6 ask whether EFCU employees are courteous, helpful, professional, and available. All items use a five-point agreement scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). Table Table 1 presents each item’s mean and the proportion selecting agree or strongly agree.

View R code to reproduce this table 
r3_items <- tibble(Question = c("Q3 Courteous", "Q4 Helpful", "Q5 Professional", "Q6 Available"),
                   Variable = c("q3", "q4", "q5", "q6")) |>
  mutate(N = map_int(Variable, ~sum(!is.na(dat[[.x]]))),
         Mean = map_dbl(Variable, ~mean(dat[[.x]], na.rm = TRUE)),
         SD = map_dbl(Variable, ~sd(dat[[.x]], na.rm = TRUE)),
         `Agree / strongly agree %` = map_dbl(Variable, ~100 * mean(dat[[.x]] >= 4, na.rm = TRUE))) |>
  select(-Variable)
knitr::kable(r3_items, digits = 2)

Finding. Employee ratings are consistently positive. Courtesy is rated most favorably (89.8% agree or strongly agree; mean 4.35). Availability has the lowest favorable response rate (78.9%; mean 4.08), suggesting that employee accessibility deserves attention even though most responses are favorable.

R3 — One-sample t-test

The composite att_employee averages Q3–Q6. A one-sample t-test compares its observed mean with 3, the neutral midpoint.

Table 2. One-sample t-test for employee proficiency, compared with 3

N Mean SD Benchmark t value df p value
128 4.21 0.59 3 22.95 127 < .001
View R code to reproduce this table 
knitr::kable(one_t(dat$att_employee, 3), digits = 3)

Interpretation. The composite average is 4.21 out of 5, significantly above the neutral value (t(127) = 22.95, p < .001). Members generally see EFCU staff as courteous, helpful, professional, and available. The relatively weaker rating for availability identifies a practical area for improvement without undermining the broadly positive assessment.

R6 — How effectively is EFCU operated?

R6 — Statement delivery and confidentiality

R6 was addressed in the earlier IC11-2 report. The descriptive findings remain relevant here: 77.3% said account statements arrive about as often as they should (Q9), and 95.3% rated statement accuracy as good or excellent (Q10r). Of those giving a valid yes/no answer, 97.7% said statements are easy to understand (Q11); 69.8% believed their account information is kept confidential (Q12).

Note that Q12 includes a no-answer response (code 3) for 22 respondents. These are excluded rather than treated as negative answers. Q9 is best described by its categories rather than treating its numerical coding as a satisfaction score.

                                               r6_questions$Benchmark[i]))
})
knitr::kable(r6_tests, digits = 3)

Interpretation. Overall management receives a strong mean rating (4.07/5), followed by beliefs that personnel protect financial information (3.95/5). Prompt loan processing (3.72), adequacy of services (3.59), and simple loan applications (3.46) are also rated above the scale midpoint (all p < .001). However, the separate yes/no confidentiality question is less reassuring: only 69.8% of valid respondents answered yes. EFCU should investigate that discrepancy before concluding that privacy concerns are resolved.

R4a — Employee proficiency by residence

R4a — Descriptive comparison and independent-samples t-test

The two groups are independent: headquarters-area residents (Q38 = 1) and members elsewhere (Q38 = 2). We compare their mean att_employee scores using Welch’s independent-samples t-test (the method demonstrated in Step 2-2, which does not assume equal group variances).

Table 5. Employee proficiency by residence: Welch independent t-test

Question HQ N HQ mean HQ SD Other N Other mean Other SD Mean difference t value df p value
att_employee 57 4.28 0.57 71 4.14 0.61 0.14 1.3 122.49 0.195
View R code to reproduce this table 
knitr::kable(by_residence("att_employee"), digits = 3)

Interpretation. Headquarters-area members averaged 4.28, compared with 4.14 among those living elsewhere. Although the headquarters group rated employees a little more positively, this small difference was not statistically significant (Welch p = .195). We therefore do not have strong evidence of a residence-based difference in employee evaluations.

Interpretation. The descriptive rates vary from service to service, but none of the 14 chi-square comparisons reaches the .05 significance level. For example, awareness of MasterCard credit cards (Q17r) is 82.5% among headquarters-area members and 93.0% elsewhere, but that apparent gap is not statistically conclusive (p = .150). Both groups are 100% aware of line-of-credit loans (Q26r); the Q26r test therefore reflects the split between use and non-use rather than an awareness gap.

Some individual response categories have small expected counts, particularly for less-used services. These chi-square results should therefore be interpreted cautiously; the descriptive percentages remain useful for understanding the sample.

Interpretation. Most operational ratings are similar between the two areas. Loan-application simplicity (Q34) stands out: members living near headquarters averaged 3.28, compared with 3.61 elsewhere (Welch t = −2.28, p = .025). About 49.1% of headquarters-area members agreed that applications are simple, versus 64.8% of other-area members. This is a useful signal that EFCU could examine the application experience more closely among headquarters-area members. However, Q34 is one of several comparisons, so the isolated result needs replication rather than a causal explanation.

Interpretation. For Q11, favorable answers are nearly universal in both groups (96.5% in the headquarters area and 98.6% elsewhere). For Q12, the yes percentages are 63.3% and 75.4%, respectively. Neither difference is statistically significant (Q11 p = .435; Q12 p = .173). Q11 has very few negative responses, which makes its chi-square approximation less reliable; results for that item should be considered descriptive.

Conclusions and recommendations

EFCU is viewed favorably overall, especially for employee courtesy and general management. Employee availability remains the comparatively weaker part of R3, so scheduling and responsiveness may deserve attention. In R6, most operations receive favorable ratings, but the mixed answers to the direct confidentiality question suggest that clear privacy communication may be helpful.

Across locations, the data provide no convincing overall evidence that employee evaluations or service-awareness patterns differ. The clearest exploratory difference concerns loan-application simplicity, which received lower scores among headquarters-area members. EFCU could examine how members in each area apply for loans and follow up with the members who found the process difficult.

Limitations. The file has 128 survey records; sample sizes vary when answers are missing. Multiple comparisons increase the chance of an isolated small p-value. Some chi-square tables have sparse cells. Statistical differences here describe associations among respondents, not proof that residence caused their views. The earlier factor and reliability analyses are not reproduced, as instructed.
